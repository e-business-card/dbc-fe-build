Digital Business Cards for Mortgage Brokers and Financial Advisors

IndustriesNov 26·By Ilyes Tounsi, founder

For loan officers, brokers and financial advisors, a digital card is mostly about control: one page where your title, firm and links all stay current. This post covers what to put on it, and where we can't help you.

One thing first. This isn't legal or compliance advice, and we don't know what applies to your state, your country, your firm or your license type. Where a rule comes up below, the answer is always the same: ask the person at your firm who decides.

Why a card page helps in regulated work

These jobs share a pattern. You carry identifiers that must stay correct, and the wording you're allowed to use gets updated by someone else. A printed card freezes all of that on the day it was printed. A card page lets you update the text once, and every code and link you've ever handed out opens the new version.

What the card has fields for, and what it doesn't

The card has fields for your name, title, organization, phone numbers, email, address, a web address and a free-text summary. It does not have fields for an NMLS number, a CRD number, a license number or a disclosure statement. Nothing on the card checks that what you write is complete or correct.

So you add those details yourself as plain text:

  • Your ID numbers. Put them in your title or in the summary, written the way your firm asks. They then show to everyone who opens the card.
  • Disclosures. Write the approved wording into the summary. It displays as one block of text. There's no separate disclosures section, so keep it short and put the full version on a page you link to.
  • Documents. The card has one web address with its own label. Point it at a page that holds your disclosures, a relationship summary or a form your firm provides, then mention the other documents by name in the summary.
  • Your firm and branch. Use the organization field, and change it the day you move.

Get approval before you hand it out

Whether your firm treats a business card as advertising, and whether a digital version needs sign-off, is your firm's decision and sometimes your regulator's. Show your compliance contact the card page and the printed piece before you use either. Then keep the approved wording in a note, so the next edit is a copy and paste of text you know is approved, not a rewrite from memory.

We make no claims about regulatory fit. The card shows the text you give it. What that text must say is for you and your firm.

Editing once, and what a saved contact does

When a rule changes or you change jobs, you edit the card one time. The link and the QR code stay the same, so a code on an old flyer opens the current card. If you move firms, the web address of your card goes with you once your new firm has approved the wording, and so do its scan counts.

There's one limit to understand clearly. When a visitor taps Download vCard, their phone saves a copy of your details at that moment. That copy doesn't update when you edit your card. For anyone who saved your contact before a change, the old details stay in their phone. The card page is always current. A saved contact never is. If accuracy matters to you, give people the link as well and point them to the card page.

Capturing contact details at events

Referral relationships, whether with realtors, accountants or attorneys, often start in a room. With a paid plan, the lead capture form can ask people who scan your code for a name, an email, a phone number, a company and a short message. You choose which fields appear and whether the form can be skipped.

Keep it to contact details. The form isn't a place for account numbers, income, social security numbers or anything that belongs in your firm's approved secure channel. A simple message prompt such as "How can I help?" is enough. Confirm with your firm that collecting contact details this way is acceptable, because that's a firm-by-firm question.

Submitted leads go into your Contacts list, where you can add notes and tags, set a status (new, contacted, converted or archived), and export everything as a CSV. A note like "referred by a realtor at the spring mixer" will matter when you look back over where your business came from. There's no built-in link to a loan origination system or a CRM, so the CSV is how a list moves into one. If your firm has rules about where client data may be stored, check them before you export anything.

What goes on a printed piece

Keep the printed side plain: your name, your title, the QR code and a line that says what it does. Follow whatever your firm requires for printed pieces, and put the rest on the page behind the code, where one edit updates everything. On size, margin and contrast, the print rules in our guide to a QR code on a Canva flyer apply to any printed piece.

The pages for mortgage professionals and financial advisors show how a card for each can read. When you're ready to build your own, start with a free card, take it to your compliance contact, and go from there.
