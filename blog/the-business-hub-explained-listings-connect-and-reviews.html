The Business Hub Explained: Listings, Connect and Reviews

ProductNov 26·By Ilyes Tounsi, founder

The Digital QR Card Business Hub is a free directory of businesses run by our members, where a team member reviews each listing before it goes public. This post explains how it works, which rules apply, and what it can't promise.

The Hub is new. We aren't going to tell you how much traffic it sends, how many businesses are listed or what results to expect, because we don't have numbers that would be fair to quote. What we can do is explain exactly how it behaves.

What the Hub is

The Hub lives at the directory page, and anyone can browse it without an account. Visitors can search by name or description, filter by city and filter by category. Each listing shows the business name, its category, its city if the owner gave one, a short description and a link to its website. If the owner attached one of their digital cards, there's also a View card button.

Categories come from a fixed list, so a business picks the closest fit instead of making one up. The list includes realtors, insurance, mortgage, photographers, financial advisors, trades, health and wellness, legal, creative services, software and an "other" option.

Listing your business

Listing is free. You need a Digital QR Card account with a confirmed email address. The form asks for:

  • Your business name.
  • A category.
  • A city (optional).
  • A description, optional, in a sentence or two. It has a character limit, and it can't contain links or email addresses.
  • A website, which must start with https://.
  • A link to one of your cards (optional), which adds the View card button to your listing.

A free account can have one listing. An account on a paid plan can have up to three. Nothing on the Hub speeds up or buys an approval.

How review works

When you submit, your listing goes into a queue and a person on our team reviews it, including the website you linked to. Until it's approved, it isn't visible in the directory. You'll see one of four statuses on your own listings: pending review, live, not approved or withdrawn.

If a listing isn't approved, you're told why, and you can edit it to address the reason and send it back. Editing the name, description, website, category or city of a live listing sends it back through review, so unreviewed text never goes public. You can withdraw a listing at any time, and that frees the slot for another business.

The Pro badge

Some listings carry a Pro label. It means the owner bought a plan. A trial, a gift or a team seat does not count. It doesn't mean the business has been checked, vouched for or endorsed by us, so don't read it as a quality mark. One more effect is small: when two businesses are due on the same day for the "Business of the day" spot, which features approved listings in rotation, the paid account goes first.

Connect: asking for an introduction

Connect is how one listed business asks another for an introduction. Both listings have to be approved, and you send the request from your own approved listing.

  • You write one short message, up to 300 characters, on a single line. Links, email addresses and phone numbers aren't allowed in it.
  • You can send up to 5 new requests a day.
  • The other business sees it in its Connections inbox, and can accept or decline.
  • If they accept, both of you see the businesses as connected. Accepting shares nothing but your public listing and its card link, which they could already see. No contact details are copied anywhere.
  • If they decline, you can't ask the same business again for 30 days. If you withdraw a request, there's a 24-hour pause before you can ask them again.

The rules about contact details are there on purpose. Connect is meant to start a conversation, not to hand out phone numbers or become a way to send unwanted messages.

Reviews

Reviews work differently from a typical review site, and the rules are strict.

  • A review is written by a business, as that business, about another business it has connected with through Connect. It's shown with the reviewer's business name, never anonymously.
  • The connection must have been accepted at least 24 hours earlier. You can write one review per business.
  • A review has a rating from 1 to 5 and between 20 and 500 characters of text. No links, phone numbers or email addresses.
  • Reviewing is optional. A team member approves each review before it appears. Until then, only the author sees it.
  • The author can edit a review, which sends it back for approval, or withdraw it.
  • The reviewed business can publish one reply per review, between 20 and 300 characters. It appears right away. It can also report a review to the moderators. It can't hide or delete one.
  • A listing shows the real number of reviews. The average rating appears only once there are at least 3 reviews.

An open review box on a small directory would be easy to fill with fake or paid reviews. Limiting reviews to businesses that have connected, and approving each one, won't make the system perfect, but it keeps the reviews tied to real business relationships.

What the Hub can't do

  • It doesn't promise traffic. While the directory is small, its pages ask search engines not to index them. And the website link on a listing is marked so that search engines don't count it as an endorsement, so don't expect a listing to lift your own site in search results.
  • It doesn't promise leads. It's a place for other members to find you and for you to find them.
  • It doesn't vouch for anyone. Approval means a person reviewed the listing and its website address. It isn't a background check.

Getting a listing ready

  1. Use the name your customers know you by.
  2. Choose the category that fits what people hire you for.
  3. Add your city if you serve a local area. That's what the city filter uses.
  4. Write a plain description of what you do. Leave out links and email addresses, or the form will refuse it.
  5. Link to a website page that shows who you are, and make sure it loads over https.
  6. Attach a card if you have one, so visitors can save your contact.

Browse the Business Hub to see how listings read. If you don't have an account yet, make a free card, confirm your email, then use the List your business button.
